# Objection handling appendix

## Appendix A — Executive objections

### “How do I know the value is real?”

**Executive answer:**

> The customer-scale figures are explicitly illustrative. The decision gate is not the benchmark; it is the first two controls running against your data. We would measure gross exposure, evidence quality, duplication, dispute rate, and practical recoverability before scaling.

**Technical bridge:**

> Every measured exception must retain a source reference and named control, so the business case can be audited rather than inferred from a model score.

### “This sounds expensive.”

**Executive answer:**

> The design reuses the existing data estate and starts with two controls. The first investment is bounded, and the broader program is funded only if measured exposure justifies it.

**Technical bridge:**

> Serverless resources, SQL-first controls, and one deployable repository reduce standing infrastructure and integration overhead during the validation phase.

### “How quickly do we see value?”

**Executive answer:**

> The objective is a quantified first-value result in two to four weeks, not a full production transformation. Production scale, automation, and write-back follow the measured business case.

### “What if recovery is much lower than the headline?”

> We assume it will be lower. Gross exposure and recoverable value are different metrics. Aged claims, disputes, churn, and contractual limits reduce backward recovery, which is why prevention and early detection become increasingly important.

### “Why Databricks rather than another point solution?”

> Because the leakage already spans the customer’s governed data estate. This approach brings controls, AI, analytics, lineage, and the operational application to shared data rather than creating another silo that needs to be reconciled.

---

## Appendix B — Technical objections

### “How do identities resolve across systems?”

> Each reconciliation resolves source-system keys to golden TM Forum identities using explicit joins. That reduces first-release object count, but duplicates mapping logic. A production roadmap should standardize those mappings or introduce a governed canonical identity layer as complexity grows.

### “How do you prevent false positives?”

> Controls are named and testable, amounts must be non-negative, enumerations are constrained, documents that cannot be parsed are flagged, and seeded anomalies establish deterministic test coverage. Production rollout adds measured precision, analyst disposition feedback, thresholds, and source-specific exception policies.

### “Where is PII protected?”

> Unity Catalog is the analytical governance boundary with least-privilege grants and intended masking for sensitive identifiers. The application runs through service-principal bindings. Case assignments in Lakebase require separate operational access controls. Deployed masking and tag status must be verified in the target environment rather than assumed from design artifacts.

### “Why Lakebase instead of Delta tables for cases?”

> Cases require frequent row-level updates, notes, lifecycle constraints, and low-latency transactional behavior. Lakebase fits that workload. The trade-off is that recovery KPIs need a synchronization pattern before they participate in Unity Catalog metric views and executive dashboards.

### “Is this streaming?”

> The current design is batch-first for deterministic, auditable reconciliation. Live Lakeflow Connect or streaming ingestion is a production extension where source latency and recovery economics justify it.

### “Does Genie have unrestricted data access?”

> No. The standalone Genie space queries governed Unity Catalog data and inherits its access controls. The embedded Genie side panel is planned, not part of the current application.

### “How portable is deployment?”

> The repository defines a bundle-based deployment contract and resolves environment-specific hosts, warehouses, and resource bindings at deploy time. We do not assert a cloud provider until the customer deployment profile is confirmed.

### “What happens if the AI is wrong?”

> AI-derived signals do not bypass the control framework. They produce reviewable exceptions with source context and detection method. Analysts retain disposition and recovery responsibility, and seeded demo anomalies are not presented as proof of production model accuracy.

## Appendix F — Sources & assumptions for the illustrative economics

This appendix exists so every number on Slide 1 and Slide 7 can be traced to a dated, named source
or an explicitly labeled assumption. Three categories are used consistently throughout this
presentation, and every figure in this deck belongs to exactly one:

| Category | Meaning | Visual treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Sourced Lumen fact** | A figure taken directly from Lumen's own public filings or investor materials | Cited inline with filing name/date; no color pill |
| **External industry assumption** | A benchmark or rate taken from a named third-party (TM Forum, PwC, RAG) applied to the sourced Lumen fact | Gold "illustrative" pill on-slide; full citation here |
| **Synthetic Lakelink demo output** | A number produced by the seeded `cdm_tmforum` demo dataset (seed=42) — never Lumen's actual data | Teal "measured (demo)" pill on-slide |

### Sourced Lumen fact

- **Total revenue, fiscal year 2025: $12.402 billion.** Source: Lumen Technologies, Form 10-K for the
  year ended December 31, 2025, filed with the SEC February 20, 2026 (accession no.
  0000018926-26-000014; document `lumn-20251231.htm`). Corroborated by Lumen's press release
  "Lumen Technologies Reports Solid Fourth Quarter and Full Year 2025 Results," February 3, 2026
  (furnished as Exhibit 99.1 to Form 8-K, same accession family), which states the identical $12.402B
  figure (down from $13.108B in FY2024, a 5% decline).
- Segment detail available in the same 10-K if asked: Business segment $9.895B, Mass Markets segment
  $2.507B for FY2025.

### External industry assumption — gross leakage rate (2.0%–2.5%)

- **Lower/upper bound source:** PwC, *Optimizing revenue with data analytics: differentiate with
  data* (December 2020): "on average, data shows that 1–5% of telecommunications companies' income is
  subject to revenue leakage." Our 2.0–2.5% assumption sits inside this range, toward its lower end.
- **Corroborating historical benchmark:** TM Forum Revenue Assurance Survey 2017/18 (public PDF):
  0.9% average *measured* leakage, 1.9% average *coverage-adjusted estimated* leakage across
  respondents; top-tier RA organizations reported <0.2%.
- **Material caveat — read this on stage if challenged:** TM Forum's most recent Business Assurance
  Survey (report IG1438, "TM Forum Business Assurance Survey Report 2024–2025," published June 24,
  2025, TM Forum-approved September 5, 2025) reports the industry average has fallen further, to
  **~0.52%** (down from 1.22% in 2021), as RA practice has matured industry-wide. A separate 2020 RAG
  (Revenue Assurance and Fraud Management) survey of 175 telecom respondents found *non-fraud
  operational leakage* (billing, rating/tariff errors specifically) averaging **2.92%** of revenue —
  TM Forum's own August 2025 commentary corroborates this 2.92% figure for non-fraud billing mistakes.
  Our 2.0–2.5% assumption is therefore positioned as a **conservative-to-moderate planning test**: above
  the current all-leakage industry average (0.52%), but below the non-fraud billing-error-specific
  average (2.92%) — not a claim about what Lumen's actual leakage rate is.

### External industry assumption — recovery rate (50%–70%)

- **Source:** TM Forum Revenue Assurance Survey 2017/18: average industry recovery rate of identified
  leakage was 51%; TM Forum's 2021 retrospective article notes this had risen from 21% in 2009, with
  an increasing share of operators reporting recovery rates above 75%.
- Our 50–70% range is centered on that 51% average, with the upper bound reflecting top-tier program
  performance rather than the average.

### The formulas, shown in full

```
Gross leakage exposure  = Lumen FY2025 revenue × industry leakage-rate assumption
                         = $12.402B × 2.0%          to   $12.402B × 2.5%
                         = $248.0M                  to   $310.1M

Recovered/prevented value = Gross leakage (low end) × recovery-rate assumption (low end)
                             to Gross leakage (high end) × recovery-rate assumption (high end)
                           = $248.0M × 50%           to   $310.1M × 70%
                           = $124.0M                 to   $217.0M
```

On-slide figures round these to **$248M–$310M** (gross exposure) and **$124M–$217M** (recovered/
prevented) — do not restate the pre-2026-08 figures ($250M–$312.5M / $125M–$220M), which were computed
against an unsourced "~$12.5B" placeholder rather than Lumen's actual FY2025 10-K revenue. The
difference between the placeholder and the sourced figure is small (~$12.5B assumed vs. $12.402B
actual) precisely because the placeholder was a reasonable round-number guess — but the sourced
figure is now defensible in a way the placeholder was not.

### Synthetic Lakelink demo output (for contrast — never blend with the above)

The ~48,108 exceptions / ~$601.5M at-risk figures shown live in Demos 1–2 are outputs of the seeded
`cdm_tmforum.revenue_assurance.gold_leakage_summary` register (fixed seed=42) — entirely separate from
the Lumen-scale economics above. See `demo-artifacts/08-test-plan.md` §"Golden outputs" and
`demo-artifacts/09-runbook.md` §2.4 for the deterministic baseline this figure must reproduce.
